Myristic Acid

Skincare Ingredient Audit

HIGH COMEDOGENIC RISK

AI Cosmetic Chemist Verdict

Myristic Acid is a saturated fatty acid frequently used in facial cleansers and moisturizers as a cleansing agent or emulsifier. While it gives products a creamy texture and helps them lather, it has a high comedogenic rating of 3 out of 5. For those with oily or acne-prone skin, it presents a major risk of clogging pores and triggering both closed comedones and fungal acne breakouts.

Flagged Concerns

Comedogenic Rating: 3/5 High Clogging Risk

Myristic acid easily penetrates the follicle and mixes with your natural sebum, leading to clogged pores, blackheads, and inflamed acne lesions in sensitive individuals.

Fungal Acne Trigger Malassezia Food

As a fatty acid, myristic acid acts as a direct food source for the Malassezia yeast responsible for fungal acne, making it completely unsafe for anyone battling this condition.

Barrier Stripping Dehydration Risk

When used in high concentrations in foaming cleansers, it can be overly stripping to the skin barrier, causing reactive oil production which further worsens acne.
